#  Direct Democracy

##  What is Direct Democracy

Direct Democracy is a form of democracy in which people vote directly on specific laws and policies rather than electing representatives to make decisions on their behalf. 

##  How Direct Democracy Works

The general process is very simple...
1. A Member of Society raises a petition asking for some change to be made
2. The Petition is published
3. If the Petition reaches a pre-defined minimum threshold then it advanced forwards and becomes a Proposed Change
4. The Proposed Change is then expanded and elaborated to include any additional details (e.g. costs, implementation plan, etc.) that might be relevant to the electorate when making their decision to support or reject the Change Proposal.
5. Proposed Change is scheduled for wider vote by all Members of Society
6. Voting takes place
7. If the Proposal is approved then the Proposal becomes policy that must be enacted by Government.

Each stage of the process would have a fixed timescale and some pre-defined thresholds that must be achieved

##  Benefits of Direct Democracy

The benefits of this particular system when compared to other Democratic Processes are...
- It gives citizens a more direct say on the agreement or rejection of legislation between general elections
- It produces more accountable government
- It generates greater civic trust
- It provides more long-term political stability
- It reduces political disillusionment
- It forces Parliament to reflect genuine public will
- It minimises political interference and influence from external interests
- It simplifies the complexity of government by reducing decisions down to individual votes on a single issue at a time

This can be done through electronic voting systems that allow people to vote on issues from the comfort of their own homes.

##  The Scope Of Direct Democracy

Obviously the entire electorate cannot be consulted for every little decision that needs to be made. 
This would remain the responsibility of the representatives elected to government.


##  Issues With Direct Democracy

On one of the on-line forums I frequent someone posted the following comment in response my advocating Direct Democracy as the solution to avoiding partizan politics... 

```text
    In that making decisions about specific acts of legislation (as opposed to general direction of policy) requires...
    1.   the ability to scrutinise how those acts are written with attention to detail, 
    2.  specialist knowledge or a willingness to consult, listen to and understand those who have it, 
    3.  and at the very least a considerable investment of time (which is why being an MP is a job and paid as such). 
    The majority of people would have few or none of these qualities and would just make a kneejerk vote based on whether the legislation was on their "side". 
    They would be able to see beyond that to the nuance of what's included, what's omitted, how exactly it's framed and what any unintended consequences might be. 
    And Dunning-Kruger being what it is, it would be the stupidest people who have the least awareness of how profoundly unfit they are for the job, while the ones who are actually are quite capable might abstain from many votes due to awareness of their limitations. 
    Sounds like a great way to lower the quality of legislative decision making.
```
Ignoring the condescending attitude of the comment the commentator does make some valid points about engagement but I'd counter-argue that most of those criticisms would equally apply to our current Elected Representative system and that, in the case of First Past The Post elections, the existing problems are much worse.

It's certainly the case that some people will abstain from voting (usually around 40% in a general election) and many of those that do vote tend to vote for the same political party every time no matter what that party's policies are.

However, once elected I think tribal voting is even worse in Parliament than it would be if the electorate were voting themselves. 
The Party Whip's pretty much enforce voting on party lines no matter what the individual MP may think.

The main reason I support Direct Democracy is that it deals with significant national issues one at a time. 
With Party Manifestos you get a package of policies and if you vote for that party then you, in effect, accept all those proposals even if you only agree with a few of them. In addition, you are effectively rejected all the proposals made by all the other Political Parties even though you may agree with some of them.

Our current way of doing things is basically an all or nothing system!

So, I know that Direct Democracy is not a perfect system (perfection does not exist in the real world) but I do honestly think it's a better system than the one we currently have.
