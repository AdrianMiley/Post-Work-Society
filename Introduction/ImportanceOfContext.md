#  The Importance Of Context

On of the reasons I tend not to reproduce verbatim quotes from academic works is that context matters when understanding the meaning of some statement.

For example, in a conversation as a reply to a point I was making about democracy in the UK someone posted these two quotes by Karl Marx...

>   _The emancipation of the working classes must be conquered by the working classes themselves; that, the struggle for the emancipation of the working classes means not a struggle for class privileges and monopolies, but for equal rights and duties, and the abolition of all class rule"_

... and this...

>   _What all socialists understand by anarchism is this: as soon as the goal of the proletarian movement, the abolition of classes, is attained, the power of the State... will disappear and governmental [or political] functions will be transformed into simple administrative functions"._

Both of these quotes may well have been reasonable 150 years ago but things have moved on quite a bit since then and most of the social conditions that Marx was reacting to are do not exist nowadays.

"Emancipation" means "the liberation of a group of people from oppression or discrimination" which, back in 1850, might well have been an accurate classification of Women and most Working Class Men (who didn't own property of sufficient value) who could not vote and hence had no say in who was elected to parliament.
However, both women and working class men have the vote in most modern democracies so "emancipation" of both have been achieved and if democracy works as intended (a big "if") then "...equal rights and duties, and the abolition of all class rule" has already been achieved.

Whether we can then also say that "...the power of the State... will disappear and governmental functions will be transformed into simple administrative functions" is a very arguable conclusion.

There was also a much clearer delineation in the 1850's between those that had property and those that did not and those that had the power to make nationally important decisions and those that did not.

So quite clearly those kind of contextual quotes can no longer be taken verbatim because the modern context is significantly different.
Consequently, I much prefer a more modern less literal interpretation of those early works.

That's not to say that all historical quotes are meaningless in the modern world but they do need to be considered in context.
